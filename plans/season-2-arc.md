# The Voyage: Season 2 Story Arc

## What Season 2 is about

Season 2 is the season where the pilot is taken, used against everyone he loves, and has to live with it through a war.

Every season of the saga has had a fear underneath it. The Tenretni were built from the fear of loss. The Foregone were built from the fear of surprise. Season 2's enemy is built from the fear of ending. They take minds that can imagine because they're trying to imagine a way past something they've seen coming. They aren't wrong that it's coming. That's what Season 3 is.

The season adapts nine Star Trek stories, in an order that builds:

| Ch | Title | Adapts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Glass Crown | The Best of Both Worlds I |
| 2 | The Silence at the Choir | The Best of Both Worlds II |
| 3 | The Listener | Emissary |
| 4 | Seven Bands | Chain of Command I and II |
| 5 | Leaving the Concourse | Call to Arms |
| 6 | The Long Way Back | Sacrifice of Angels |
| 7 | The Forged Forecast | In the Pale Moonlight |
| 8 | The Chonga Breaks | Tears of the Prophets |
| 9 | What We Leave | What You Leave Behind |
| 10 | Three Clocks | All Good Things |

The structure keeps "How history works" from Season 1. The big events are fixed: the Choir falls, the Concourse is lost and retaken, someone dies at the Chonga. What you decide changes who, how, and what's left.

Season 2 should be the most visually ambitious thing in the game. Every chapter below names its set piece: the image the chapter is built to deliver.

## What carries in from Season 1

Season 2 reads the Season 1 record. These are the choices that change it most.

| Season 1 outcome | What it changes in Season 2 |
| --- | --- |
| The Foregone came out of Ch 13 **blind** or **curious** | Blind: the Remnant is most of what's left of them, desperate for any forecast, and the forged forecast in Ch 7 is cruelly easy to sell. Curious: the Remnant is a small splinter, the rest of the Foregone are a possible ally, and Ch 7 is about winning them over. |
| Ione stayed, or didn't | If she stayed, she's the person the pilot loses at the Choir, while he's leading the attack. If she didn't, the loss is the Concourse dockmaster, who taught him the market. That cuts less deep, and the chapter is written for that. |
| A captain was lost | Every role moves along. If Lux is gone, Vask takes command in Ch 2. If Marrow is gone, the death at the Chonga falls on whoever is left who believed in it, and with nobody left, on the Pulsar Choir's eldest. With no captains at all, the pilot carries Ch 2 alone from inside the glass. |
| The swarm builders were freed or contracted (S1 Ch 8) | Freed: they arrive at the Long Way Back unasked, and their ships are the turning point of the battle. Contracted: the Claudians encased them, and you fight what they've become. |
| Lux signed the forecast (S1 Ch 6) | The Claudians find the light ship through the contract. Part of Ch 1 is learning how they knew. |
| A world was sold or recompiled (S1 Ch 11) | A recompiled world is in the war as a place the Claudians can reach. A sold world turns up in Ch 10, in the hands of whoever bought it. |

## Chapters 1 to 5: the pilot taken, the Concourse lost

1. **The Glass Crown.** A routine shuttle trip in the freighter finds a world that has been turned to glass. The Claudians arrive while the pilot is on the surface and take him. The chapter's second half is played as Lux on the light ship's bridge. It's the first time you play as a captain. The last scene: a transmission from the glass crown, in the pilot's voice, telling the light ship what it's going to do. Lux gives the order to fire, and the screen cuts to black.
    - Set piece: the glass world, a planet of clear glass with its sun's light caught inside it, cities frozen mid-motion and visible through the crust.
    - Fixed: the pilot is taken. Yours: who came along on the trip (the bay roster), and whether they get back to the bay.
2. **The Silence at the Choir.** The shot doesn't stop the crown. With the pilot's knowledge of the light ship and the Concourse, the Claudians meet the Concourse fleet at the Pulsar Choir and destroy it. You play Lux through the battle. Then you play the pilot from inside the glass, walking his own memories while the collective uses them, looking for the one thing he can imagine that it can't. Vask counts the moment, and Lux boards the crown to pull him out.
    - Set piece: the battle at the Choir, lit only by the pulsar's beam. Every sweep strobes the whole fleet into view for an instant, then dark, then the next sweep shows what's changed.
    - Fixed: the Choir falls, and the pilot leads the attack that does it. Ione dies there (or the dockmaster; see the carry-in table).
    - Decision: Lux, holding the trigger with the pilot aboard the crown. Fire, or board.
    - Cost: a shard of glass grows from the freighter's pilot seat and never comes out. The pilot's own look is unchanged.
3. **The Listener.** Grief. The pilot takes the freighter alone into the Chonga. The beings there live in all their moments at once, and to them the pilot is a strange thing that moves through time in one direction. They show him his life as rooms standing side by side: the Emberdeep cathedral, the patio, the cottage in the sun, the dive, the Choir. He keeps walking back to the Choir. They don't understand why. Explaining it to them is how he understands it himself.
    - Set piece: every place from the saga standing at once in the Chonga's light, rebuilt from the game's own scenes. Walking from one to the next is walking through your own playthrough.
    - Fixed: the Pulsar Choir names him the Listener. Yours: whether he accepts it. Accepting binds him to the Chonga, and that comes due in Ch 9.
4. **Seven Bands.** On a mission behind Remnant lines, the pilot is captured. His interrogator holds up the lamp and asks how many bands it has. There are six. The interrogator says there are seven. At the same time the Marshal takes command of the light ship, and you play the crew deck under someone this crew doesn't trust.
    - You play: the interrogation as gameplay. The lamp cycles its bands, and the HUD starts showing a seventh that isn't there. You're answering what you see, while the screen tells you you're wrong.
    - Science: "impossible colours", real hues the eye can be made to see after staring at a strong one, used by the interrogator as the seventh band.
    - Set piece: a cell lit by one band at a time, then by a colour that doesn't exist.
    - Fixed: he's freed in an exchange. Yours: whether he said seven. The Remnant remember it in Ch 8.
5. **Leaving the Concourse.** The Claudians and the Remnant gather at the Concourse. It can't be held. Before the light ship withdraws, it seeds the mouth of the Chonga so no fleet can follow through. The crew splits: some stay to resist from the Concourse's lower decks, the rest go.
    - Set piece: the Concourse's rings going dark one at a time as the light ship pulls away, the market's lights caught in the light ship's cells.
    - Fixed: the Concourse falls. Yours: who stays behind. Vask, the miner, is the natural choice to lead a resistance in the lower decks, but it's yours to decide.

## Chapters 6 to 10: the war, the Chonga, the anomaly

6. **The Long Way Back.** The light ship and every ally it has fight their way back to the Concourse, while the resistance inside tries to stop the Claudians clearing the Chonga's mouth for reinforcements. Neither side can count on the other in time. The resistance fails, and a Claudian fleet pours into the Chonga. The pilot flies the freighter in after it and asks the beings there to stop it. They do, for a price: one day the pilot won't come back from them.
    - Set piece: a thousand glass crowns entering the Chonga and never arriving. The light folds them away while the pulsar strobes.
    - You play: the battle in the light ship, sketching moves as in Season 1 Ch 10, then the inside of the Chonga with the resistance, as whoever stayed in Ch 5.
    - Fixed: the Concourse is retaken. Yours: who of the resistance lives, and which allies came (the swarm builders, if you freed them).
7. **The Forged Forecast.** The war is going badly. The pilot needs the Foregone in it, so Lux forges a forecast showing the Claudians turning on them. Each step to make it convincing makes the next one easier. When an envoy is about to expose the forgery, the envoy's ship is lost, and the pilot works out that Lux arranged it.
    - You play: the pilot alone in his quarters on the crew deck, recording a log, with each step of the plan played as he describes it. This is the first chapter told by the pilot's log.
    - Set piece: the forged forecast itself, a future rendered in glass the way the Foregone show forecasts, walked through and false.
    - Fixed: the Foregone enter the war. Yours: whether the pilot ever tells them the truth. Telling them is permanent and cools the alliance for the rest of the saga.
8. **The Chonga Breaks.** The offensive into Claudian space is finally working. At the same moment the Remnant's leader reaches the Chonga with Claudian glass and seals its mouth inside it. The beings inside go silent, the wormhole closes, and Marrow, who was in the choir hall, is killed. The Pulsar Choir's faith breaks with it.
    - Set piece: the Chonga freezing over in glass from the rim inward, the pulsar's beam sweeping through it one last time and then across an empty ring.
    - Decision: before it happens, the pilot chooses where to be: leading the offensive, or at the Chonga. Marrow dies either way. Being at the offensive wins the war faster. Being at the Chonga means he's with her at the end.
    - If the pilot said "seven" in Ch 4, this is how the Remnant found their way to the Chonga: through what they learned from breaking him.
    - Vask carries the grief from here on.
9. **What We Leave.** The last battle, at the Claudians' crown world. The war ends when the crew learns why the Claudians took minds: they've seen something coming that ends everything, and they needed enough imagination to find a way past it. It ends in terms, not in ruin. Then the Chonga's price comes due. The pilot has to go into it, outside time, and the crew has to say goodbye just as they were picturing life after the war.
    - Set piece: every encased mind released from the crown at once, a storm of colour leaving the glass and scattering across the sky.
    - You play: the goodbyes, unhurried. The dining lounge, the crew deck, the bay. Then the walk into the light.
    - Fixed: the war ends, and the pilot goes. Yours: the terms of peace, and what the Claudians are allowed to keep.
10. **Three Clocks.** Inside the Chonga, the pilot moves between three times: the home system before the Tenretni came, with the buddy who warned him; the present, on the light ship's bridge; and a future aboard the light ship without him in it. In every one there's a patch of sky where the stars are missing, and it's larger in the past than in the present. The beings in the Chonga put the pilot on trial: is his kind of mind worth carrying into what's coming?
    - You play: the three times woven together, each with its own clock, as in the dive in the first light ship chapter. A move in one time changes the others.
    - Set piece: the three skies side by side, the dark patch in each, and the last image: the helm chart on the bridge, with a region where the stars are going out, and growing.
    - The verdict: the trial isn't decided by what he's done. It's decided by whether he can change how he thinks. It's the season's last test.
    - It ends with the pilot back on the bridge, and the crew finding out what the Claudians were running from.

## Toward Season 3

The patch where the stars go out is a bubble of **false vacuum decay**, and Season 3 is the universe's ending, coming at nearly the speed of light.

This is the one universe-ending threat physics actually takes seriously. Measurements of the Higgs boson and the top quark suggest that empty space may not be in its lowest energy state. If a small region ever dropped into the lower state, it would form a bubble whose wall expands at very nearly the speed of light. Inside it, the laws of physics change, and atoms as we know them can't hold together. Published estimates put the expected wait for it to start anywhere in our observable universe far beyond the universe's current age; one 2018 calculation (Andreassen, Frost and Schwartz) gave about 10^139 years. That figure is from recall, not checked here. So the threat is real physics, and having it happen is the fiction.

One piece of real physics makes it a story problem: something moving at nearly light speed can't be seen coming. Its wall arrives almost with the last light from whatever it destroyed. That's why only the Chonga, outside time, could show it to the pilot, and why the Claudians, who knew, had no way to prove it. The dark patch on the chart in Ch 10 is the Chonga's doing, not something a telescope could see.

Season 2 seeds three questions for Season 3 to answer:

- **What started it? Tiny black holes, built to mine time.** This rests on real, published physics. In 2015 and 2016, Philipp Burda, Ruth Gregory and Ian Moss showed that small black holes can act as seeds for vacuum decay, making a bubble enormously more likely to form at the hole than in empty space ([Burda, Gregory and Moss, 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07331); [2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02152)). They concluded that either tiny black holes don't exist in our universe, or new physics keeps the vacuum stable. We're still here, so nature doesn't seem to make them. In the saga, people did.
    - The chain runs through the whole story. Ticks are banked time, mined deep in gravity. Neutron stars were slow, so somebody found a cheaper, deeper mine: manufactured micro black holes, which slow clocks hard and give off power as they evaporate. That's the "bigger mine" behind the cheap Foregone ticks that flooded the Concourse in Season 1 Ch 6.
    - A micro black hole gets more dangerous as it evaporates and shrinks. Every mine was a small chance. Enough mines over enough time made it a certainty. One of them seeded the bubble.
    - So the end of the universe was caused by an economy. Nobody wanted it; everybody's demand for time paid for it. The Claudians worked it out, and they took imaginative minds because nobody would believe a forecast of it.
    - Real versus fiction: black-hole seeding is a real theoretical result. Later papers have argued over how strong the effect is; I know of that debate from recall, not from checking it here. Manufactured micro black holes, time mines and the timeline are the saga's invention.
- **Can it be stopped, outrun or survived?** Physics says not outrun; nothing does better than lightspeed. The light ship goes where the pilot imagines, though, and that rule has never been tested against the end of space itself.
- **What kind of civilization do you want to become when there may be no future?** The Star Trek question again, at its hardest.

## Build notes and open questions

New things Season 2 asks the game to do:

| System | First chapter | Built on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Playing as a captain, not the pilot | 1 | `capitalWalk`, the bridge, `chapter.crew()` |
| Glass that holds light: refraction, caught colour, encased figures | 1 | A new material; the pilot's own model stays unchanged underneath |
| A battle lit only by a sweeping pulsar beam | 2 | `fleets.js`, a strobe light rig |
| The Chonga: past places from the saga rebuilt side by side | 3 | The existing sites, loaded together under one light |
| A HUD that lies | 4 | `lampHud.js` |
| A chapter told through the pilot's log | 7 | `cineTalk`, the crew deck quarters |
| Three times played at once | 10 | The three clocks of the dive |

The strongest objection is that Season 2 borrows its spine from nine of the most famous stories in Star Trek. Someone who knows them will see each beat coming. The plan answers that by changing the reason for each beat, not only the names. The Borg wanted perfection; the Claudians are running from the end of the universe. The Prophets were gods; the Chonga are a people who've never had to grieve. Still, if a chapter starts to feel like a retelling, change the beat, not just the dressing.

The second risk is weight. This season kills Marrow and Ione, takes the pilot for two chapters, and makes him lead a massacre. That's the point of it, but nothing in Season 1 is this dark. Ch 3 and Ch 9 are where the season breathes. Don't cut them for scope.

Season 2 can't be built until Season 1 is, because nearly every chapter reads the Season 1 record.

Decided:

- The woman in the cottage garden stays unexplained.
- Ch 8 and Ch 10 aren't sealed.
- The vacuum decay was seeded by micro black holes built as time mines.

Open questions:

- [ ] Should the crew find out the cause in Season 2's last chapter, or save it for Season 3?
- [ ] The future in Ch 10 is shown without the pilot in it, so his look never changes. Is that the right call, or does the owner want to see an older pilot?
- [ ] Final names for the Remnant and the Marshal.
